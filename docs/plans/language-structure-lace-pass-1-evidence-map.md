# 9. Feedback and the "proving relational ground"

If outbound responses become pointable events on the same immutable history, then a future reader could prove bounded historical propositions such as:

- input event (I) preceded output event (O);
- (O) was generated with named support (S) if that support is recorded/provenanced;
- later input/event (F) followed (O);
- the same witnessed structural pattern recurred at other positions;
- a candidate response reused a named prior structural pattern;
- the actual sequence differed from a predicted candidate.

It **cannot** prove merely from order:

- that (O) was semantically correct;
- that (O) caused (F);
- that the highest-ranked pattern was the optimal one;
- that the human's interpretation equals the device's local interpretation;
- that a verifier/model score is truth.

### Operational interpretation of "proving ground"

A safe formulation is:

> One immutable Lace may provide a replayable **evidence ground** for comparing structural predictions and actual interaction history, provided the interaction crossings and their support are pointable.

That is stronger and more precise than calling every later reaction "proof that the answer was right."

# 11. RM-A attack: did the hypothesis solve it?

No.

It does, however, show that the old name **retain-when** is carrying two different kinds of questions:

```
A. EVENT ADMISSION
   Did an external crossing actually occur, and how is that crossing represented?

B. STRUCTURAL PARTICIPATION
   Given structural evidence inside/over Lace, when is a new Join appended?
```

For successful inbound WORD arrival, A is already closed at the admitted-arrival layer.

For outbound response, A is open.

For RootTouch, B is already closed.

For other sampling-derived relations, B is RM-A and remains open.

A future event-oriented architecture could reduce how often B is needed if responses themselves carry already-selected relational structure. But then the selection rule lives in the reader/emitter and must still satisfy the same no-semantic-chooser, provenance, and replay constraints. Moving the selector is not eliminating the selector.

# 12. Mechanism A vs B attack

The new language/I-O/Star-search architecture does **not** choose between Fable's two surviving mechanisms.

### Mechanism A
Eagerly collapses every remaining frontier element until one covering point remains.

### Mechanism B
Chains once to the most recent other frontier element and stops.

Both remain compatible with:
- one append-only history;
- dictionary seed;
- derived Star search;
- response feedback;
- device-local matching/ranking.

A reader can search a branching history without requiring the final tip to reach every row.

### H2 remains the cheapest discriminator

H2's exact recorded human sentence says the (n+1) grounding "grounds words together scaling up until 1 point represents the entire lace."

But current traceability classifies H2 as a historical grounding-requirement precursor absorbed/refined by H5/H6, and H5 is explicitly a **capability**, not a chooser.

Therefore this pass cannot lawfully infer:

```
every active frontier MUST collapse to one point
```

from H2 without a clarification or a new specimen.

**AB-1 remains UNDECIDED.**
